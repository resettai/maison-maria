Reading a sunscreen label

Five things on the back of the bottle tell you more than the number on the front.

October 2026 · 5 min read

A European sunscreen label is a regulated document, and because MARIA is made to the European rules wherever it is sold, this is the label you will find on every bottle. Once you know what each mark means, you can judge any bottle in a shop in under a minute.

1. The SPF category

The EU allows eight numbers: 6, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 50 and 50+. Anything else printed on a European product is not compliant. "Low" is 6 to 10, "medium" 15 to 25, "high" 30 to 50 and "very high" is 50+ only. "Sunblock", "total protection" and "all-day protection" are not permitted claims, because no sunscreen does those things.

2. The UVA circle

The letters UVA inside a circle mean the UVA protection factor is at least one third of the SPF, and the critical wavelength is 370 nanometres or more. Without the circle, you do not know what the product does against UVA. All MARIA sunscreens carry it.

3. Water resistance

"Water resistant" means at least half the SPF remains after two 20-minute swims, tested to ISO 16217. "Very water resistant" means the same after four. Neither means waterproof, and towelling removes sunscreen faster than water does. Reapply after both.

4. The open-jar symbol

A small open jar with "12M" means the product keeps for twelve months after opening. UV filters degrade; a sunscreen from two summers ago may still feel the same and protect far less. Write the opening month on the base.

5. The ingredient list

Filters appear by their INCI names. On a MARIA fluid you will find Bis-Ethylhexyloxyphenol Methoxyphenyl Triazine (Tinosorb S), Methylene Bis-Benzotriazolyl Tetramethylbutylphenol (Tinosorb M), Diethylamino Hydroxybenzoyl Hexyl Benzoate (Uvinul A Plus) and Ethylhexyl Triazone (Uvinul T150). You will not find Octocrylene, Homosalate or Benzophenone-3. Why is a story of its own.
