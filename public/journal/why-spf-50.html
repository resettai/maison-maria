Why SPF 50+, and not 30

The difference between 30 and 50 looks small on paper. On skin, applied the way people actually apply sunscreen, it is not.

October 2026 · 4 min read

SPF 30 filters about 97 percent of UVB. SPF 50 filters about 98 percent. Put like that, the extra twenty points look like marketing. The number that matters is the one that gets through: 3 percent against 2 percent, which is a third less burning radiation reaching the skin over a day outside.

The real argument is about quantity. SPF is measured in a laboratory at 2 milligrams of product per square centimetre of skin. Study after study finds that people apply between a quarter and a half of that. At half the dose, protection does not halve; it collapses. An SPF 30 applied at half the tested amount behaves closer to an SPF 5 or 6. An SPF 50+ applied the same way still lands in the teens.

That is why every MARIA face fluid is formulated to the highest category the EU allows us to print, 50+, which means a measured SPF of at least 60. The headroom is there for the morning you are in a hurry, the afternoon you forget to reapply and the day you spend on the water.

UVA is the other half

SPF only describes protection against UVB, the wavelengths that burn. UVA goes deeper, drives most of the visible ageing of skin and contributes to melanoma risk. In the EU, a sunscreen may only carry the UVA-in-a-circle mark if its UVA protection is at least a third of its SPF. MARIA fluids are tested to that standard with the in vitro method ISO 24443 and have a critical wavelength above 370 nanometres, which is the definition of broad spectrum.

What 50+ does not do

It does not make a thin layer thick, and it does not last all day. Two full pumps for face, neck and ears, applied 15 minutes before going out, and again every two hours outdoors. The Sun Mist exists for the second part, because a reapplication you cannot do over make-up is a reapplication that does not happen.
