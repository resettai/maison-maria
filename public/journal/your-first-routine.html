Your first routine, at fourteen

Three steps, two minutes, nothing harsh. What a dermatologist would tell a teenager, and what the internet usually does not.

October 2026 · 4 min read

Skin at fourteen is doing more than it will ever do again: more oil, faster turnover, hormones arriving in waves. The instinct is to fight it with everything the algorithm suggests. The evidence says the opposite. The teenagers with the best skin at twenty are the ones who did three things consistently and nothing else.

Step one: wash, do not scrub

A gentle foaming cleanser morning and evening, and after sport instead of soap. Salicylic acid at 0.5 percent keeps pores clear without stripping; stripped skin answers with more oil, which is the loop most teenage routines are stuck in. Clear Cleanser is built for exactly this. Scrubs, brushes and hot water make it worse.

Step two: hydrate without oil

Oily skin still needs water. An oil-free gel with hyaluronic acid and niacinamide gives it that and nothing else, and sits under sunscreen without sliding. Gel Moisturiser is the one that gets used because it feels like nothing.

Step three: the habit that matters most

Daily SPF is the single decision at fourteen that shows at forty. It also stops the dark marks acne leaves from getting darker. The objection is always texture, which is why Matte Fluid SPF 50+ exists: oil-free, soft-matte, invisible, and tested on skin that breaks out. Two pumps, last step, every morning. SPF Powder or Sun Mist at lunch if the day is outdoors.

When something shows up

A hydrocolloid patch overnight, or a thin one under make-up by day. It draws the spot out and, more importantly, stops picking, which is what turns a three-day blemish into a three-year mark. For the deep ones, Blemish Gel with salicylic acid 2 percent, twice a day, and patience.

What to skip

Retinol, glycolic acid, vitamin C at 20 percent, anything with fragrance, anything that tingles. They are products for later, and most of them make teenage skin worse. If acne is painful, widespread or leaving scars, that is a doctor's job, not a routine's.

All four products together are The First Routine. Three steps, two minutes, and a card instead of a leaflet.
